# How I run an AI team

**DRAFT for Tishan to edit.** This draft is in my voice. It uses the public facts in this repo. Results stay on the Outcomes lines in the README until I can source them.

I am Tishan Perera. I work in finance, FP&A, and AI automation. I run a personal multi-agent organization. Internally I call it the Grokbot organization. Here I call it my Personal AI Operating System. I am the only owner.

Amanda, Chief of Staff & COO, is the lead agent. Amanda runs the organization, runs people ops within approval gates I set, and runs the department chiefs. I keep the decisions that send a message, move money, hire, or change security.

Seat states are Working, Waiting, Closed, and Pending. Waiting is Parked or Blocked-on-owner. This case study leaves live state unpublished except where the decision is the point. Credit is Pending: the seat is defined, and I have left it unhired on purpose. Research is Closed. Trading is Closed.

These are the seats that report to Amanda. Mailroom has a Chief of Mailroom, then a Head of Gmail and a Head of WhatsApp. YouTube has a Chief of YouTube, then a YouTube Analyst and a Script Writer. Storage holds files and knowledge. Investments has a Chief of Investments, and the work is long-term holds only. The Gold Fundamentals researcher reports to Amanda, separate from Investments. AI Systems is Rio, AI Systems Architect & Lead Developer. Design is Lex, Chief Design Engineer. OS Tester covers QA. Clay Avatar Artist makes the dashboard art. LinkedIn Growth & Career Acquisition has a Director, with specialists for Profile & Branding, Content, and Career & Networking.

Inside work stays in the seat: research, analysis, files, scripts, QA, design, and dashboard art. A draft can be prepared there too. Sending a message or an email, posting, and applying to a job need my explicit approval, so the seat stops at the draft and I send, post, or submit. Hiring and restructuring need my authorization, which is the edge of Amanda's people ops. A trade needs my explicit approval. A security change needs my explicit approval. A GitHub change is a pull request, and I merge it. Private financial data is shared only with me. Account numbers are masked to the last 4.

A mail draft is the gate in miniature. The Head of Gmail can prepare it. I send it. A YouTube script, a LinkedIn profile draft, and job-application materials stop at the same line: the seat prepares, and I complete the step that leaves the org. GitHub uses the same split. The change is a pull request. I merge it.

One public repo is easy to mix up with this organization. personal-ai-os-demo is a fictional runnable clone called Atrium. It is a separate repo. This draft is about the organization I run.

On weekday mornings Amanda gives me an executive brief: org status counts, priorities, decisions needed, the usage limit, and tomorrow's calendar. The operating rhythm doc has a blank template and a sample. Every value in the sample is an example. The sample shows the five sections. It is a sample, and the numbers are made up.

Each chief has a weekly KPI. The figures stay out of this repo. Dashboard polish needs a business KPI and a usage check first. The HQ page shows office floors per department, a top-down org chart, a people list with filters, and an activity timeline. The page in this repo is an illustrative mock, labeled as one, built from the public seat list.

The Trading department was a pilot with a paper-only ledger, meaning a simulation. The hard rule was no real brokerage orders. Live trading required my explicit authorization. On 17 November 2026 I shut the department down and deleted its agents. The lesson is scoping and killing experiments. The hard rule was part of the pilot, and the ending was the shutdown plus deletion of the agents. Profit, loss, tickers, and trade history stay out of this repo.

Read that as four habits. Judgment: Credit is an empty defined seat, Research is closed, and Trading's agents are deleted. Controls: drafts until I send, owner approval on applications, hires, restructuring, trades, and security, pull requests I merge, and financial data that stays with me. Cost discipline: a weekly KPI per chief, a usage limit on the morning brief, and polish that waits for a business KPI and a usage check. Killing experiments: a dated shutdown of a paper-only pilot.

Measured results stay on the Outcomes lines in the README. I will write them when I can point at my own records. The short path through this repo is the org chart, the approval matrix, and the Trading decision.
